# **Customer Service & Compliance Guidelines \- Call Center**

**Document ID:** COMP-NS-099  
**Department:** Risk & Compliance, National Sentinel Insurance  
**System Role:** Source of Truth for Regulatory Compliance and Fraud Detection

## **1\. Mandatory Call Disclosures**

To comply with state and federal telecommunications laws (including two-party consent states), all National Sentinel agents must read the mandatory recording disclosure verbatim at the beginning of every inbound and outbound call.

* **Script:** *"This call is being recorded for quality assurance and compliance purposes."*  
* **Failure to Disclose:** Failing to state this disclosure is a Class 1 compliance violation. If an agent realizes halfway through a call that they forgot the disclosure, they must state it immediately and document the delay in the ClaimSphere notes.

## **2\. Protected Health Information (PHI) and HIPAA**

During FNOL and Bodily Injury (BI) claims, agents will be exposed to Protected Health Information (PHI). National Sentinel Insurance acts as a covered entity under HIPAA.

* **Minimum Necessary Rule:** Agents must only collect health information that is strictly necessary to process the claim (e.g., "Were you injured?" or "Which hospital were you transported to?").  
* **Do Not Interrogate:** Do not ask for specific diagnoses, pre-existing conditions, or detailed medical histories on initial intake calls.  
* **Secure Systems:** Never write PHI on physical sticky notes or unencrypted local documents. All injury data must be entered directly into the secured ClaimSphere portal under the Medical Intake tab.

## **3\. Fraud Indicators (Special Investigations Unit \- SIU)**

Agents are the first line of defense against insurance fraud. If you detect any of the following "Red Flags" during a call, you must route a referral to the National Sentinel SIU department. **Do not confront the customer or accuse them of fraud.**  
**Common Fraud Red Flags:**

* The incident occurred less than 48 hours after the policy was purchased, or shortly after coverage limits were increased.  
* The caller is unusually calm for a severe accident, or overly aggressive and demanding an immediate payout.  
* The caller is evasive when asked for basic details about the accident location, time, or the police department involved.  
* Handwritten receipts for expensive items in the vehicle are provided immediately.  
* The caller uses highly specific insurance litigation terminology on an initial intake call.

## **4\. Forbidden Phrases ("What Not to Say")**

To protect the legal interests of National Sentinel and the policyholder, agents must strictly avoid the following language:

* **Never admit liability:** Never say, *"Since our driver hit you, we will take care of everything,"* or *"It sounds like you were at fault."* Liability is determined by the Adjuster, not the intake agent.  
* **Never promise coverage:** Never say, *"We will definitely pay for this."* Instead, say, *"I have submitted this to your adjuster for a coverage review."*  
* **Never advise on legal representation:** If a customer asks if they need an attorney, respond with: *"I cannot provide legal advice. That is a personal decision for you to make."*
