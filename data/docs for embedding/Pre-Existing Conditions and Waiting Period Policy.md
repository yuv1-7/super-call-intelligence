# **Pre-Existing Conditions & Waiting Period Policy**

**Document ID:** POL-MED-NS-002  
**Last Updated:** March 1, 2026  
**Department:** Claims Adjudication / Underwriting  
**Applies To:** All Medical Claims Personnel  
**System Role:** National Sentinel Insurance Policy Reference

## **1\. Purpose**

This document defines National Sentinel Insurance's policy on pre-existing conditions (PED) and applicable waiting periods for medical insurance claims. Agents must reference this policy when handling claims that may involve pre-existing conditions.

## **2\. Definition of Pre-Existing Condition**

A pre-existing condition is any medical condition, illness, or injury that was diagnosed, treated, or for which medical advice or treatment was recommended by a physician, within the 48 months prior to the policy inception date.

## **3\. Waiting Period Rules**

### **3.1 Standard Pre-Existing Condition Waiting Period**

* All pre-existing conditions are subject to a **4-year (48-month) waiting period** from the original policy inception date.  
* During the waiting period, any claim directly related to the pre-existing condition is **excluded from coverage**.  
* Once the waiting period has expired, the pre-existing condition is treated as any other covered illness under the standard claim process.

### **3.2 Specific Disease Waiting Period**

Certain conditions carry a separate **2-year specific disease waiting period**, regardless of whether they are pre-existing:  
* Hernia and related surgeries  
* Cataracts and lens replacement  
* Joint replacement (knee, hip, shoulder)  
* Kidney and urinary stones  
* Benign prostatic hypertrophy  
* Tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy  
* Sinusitis-related surgery

### **3.3 Day-One Coverage**

The following are covered from Day 1 of the policy (no waiting period):  
* Accidental injuries and resulting hospitalization  
* Emergency admissions for acute conditions  
* Life-threatening emergencies (heart attack, stroke, anaphylaxis)

## **4\. Non-Disclosure of Pre-Existing Conditions**

* If the insurer discovers that a pre-existing condition was not disclosed at the time of policy application, the claim may be **rejected**.  
* If non-disclosure is suspected, the claim must be flagged for **Underwriting Review** before any payment is released.  
* Agents must document the basis for suspicion in the claim notes.

## **5\. Agent Communication Guidelines**

* If the waiting period has **expired**: Process the claim normally. Do not mention the waiting period.  
* If the waiting period is **active**: Inform the policyholder clearly and sensitively that the condition may be subject to the waiting period exclusion. Offer to have the Claims Resolution team review their case if they have questions about the exclusion.  
* Never use language that implies the policyholder is at fault for having a pre-existing condition.
